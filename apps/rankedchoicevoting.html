How Ranked Choice Voting Works

Ranked choice voting (also called instant-runoff voting) lets voters rank candidates in order of preference instead of choosing only one. Votes are then counted in rounds:

  1. If a candidate wins an outright majority of first-preference votes (50% + 1), they are declared the winner.
  2. If no candidate has a majority, the candidate with the fewest first-preference votes is eliminated.
  3. Ballots for the eliminated candidate transfer to each voter's next-ranked surviving choice.
  4. A new tally is conducted to check whether any candidate now holds a majority.
  5. The process repeats, round by round, until a candidate secures a majority of the remaining votes.

A ballot that runs out of ranked surviving choices is set aside as exhausted and no longer counts toward the majority threshold.
